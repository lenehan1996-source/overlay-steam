# Playbook

Patterns, not scripts. Regenerate every line for the specific girl — a
recycled message reads as recycled. Every example below is written in his
register (see `voice.md`): short, flat, no terminal punctuation.

---

## S0 — The opener

Anchor to **one specific thing** in her profile. Openers referencing something
specific run roughly 58% reply rates against generic greetings; on Hinge a
like sent with a comment is about twice as likely to become a date, and 72% of
users say the comment itself increases their interest. Specificity is not
politeness, it's conversion.

The move is: pick the detail, then make a **claim** about it rather than ask a
question about it.

- **Photo with a location** → treat it as a shared history.
  `you went without me` · `weve been there, dont pretend`
- **She's doing something skilled** → mock-competitive.
  `im better at that than you. thats not a compliment its a challenge`
- **A pet** → negotiate for the pet, not her.
  `not interested in you, tell me about the dog`
- **Something faintly ridiculous** → the mild complaint.
  `this is a lot of effort for a tuesday`
- **A prompt answer with an opinion** → disagree, lightly and specifically.
  `wrong, and im going to need you to defend that`
- **Nothing to work with** → don't force a hook. Go pure frame, the way his
  wife line does. It works *because* it ignores the profile.

**Never**: "hey", "how's your week", "you're gorgeous", or a question she can
close in one word.

## S1 — She replied

**Low-effort reply** ("haha", "yeah", one word, no question back):

Do not raise your energy to compensate. That's the reflex and it's the error —
it tells her effort gets rewarded without being returned. **Mirror down.**
Match her length, keep the frame, no question.

> her: `haha`
> him: `i know`

Then stop. If the next one is also dead, she's PARK. If she adds something,
she was just busy — carry on.

**Good reply** (length, a question, plays along): she's in. Do not settle in
and get comfortable — this is the window. One more exchange that builds the
frame, then ask.

## S2 — Banter, and getting out of it

**S2 is where it dies.** Ask inside ~10 messages; past that, conversion drops
and the thread becomes a texting friendship that neither of you closes.

Signs it's time to ask right now:
- She's matched or beaten his last three messages for length
- She's asked him two questions
- A bit is running that both of you are enjoying
- She mentions her week, her plans, being free, being bored
- It's been more than ~8 messages and nothing has been proposed

The highest-leverage move at S2 is not a funnier line. It's a day.

## S3 — The ask

**Activity + place + day + time, stated.** Four parts, no permission.

FAST track (screening date, low cost, weeknight):
> `drinks thursday, theres a place in surry hills youll like`

BUILD track (she's earned some thought — name the thing, keep the delivery flat):
> `theres a tiny wine bar in a laneway in darlinghurst, youd like it. thursday`

Riding a frame straight into the ask, which is the strongest version:
> `good. keep it that way. drinks thursday, surry hills`

**Soft yes but no day** ("yeah we should!", "sounds fun"): that is not a yes,
it's a deferral. Pin it in the next message with a two-option close — she's
then choosing between two yeses rather than deciding whether to say one.
> `thursday or saturday`

Nothing else in that message. No cushioning.

**She's vague twice** ("I'm so busy at the moment", "let me check my week"):
stop pushing. One line that hands it back and costs him nothing, then silence.
> `let me know when your week calms down`

Do not follow up on that. If she wants it she'll surface.

**Getting off the app**: attach it to the plan, don't make it its own ask.
> `whats your number, ill send you the place`

## S4 — Locked, pre-date

Don't re-sell it. The most common own-goal between the ask and the date is
over-texting into it — every extra message is another chance to cool.

One light message the day before is plenty, and only if there's something real
to say. Then **confirm the morning of**, as a statement:

> `7 at [place], see you there`

**Never** `are we still on?` — that is an invitation to cancel, and she will
take it more often than she'd have cancelled unprompted. Confirmation texts
measurably cut flakes; the phrasing is what does it.

If there's a real gap between the ask and the date, a short call or a voice
note beats another three days of typing. Rapport before meeting is one of the
few things that reliably reduces flaking.

## Flakes

She has parallel options and a match costs her nothing. Flaking is the base
rate, not a verdict. The only losing response is showing it landed.

**Cancels with a reason, offers nothing**: one line, unbothered, re-propose
once.
> `all good. saturday then`

**Cancels twice**: she's a maybe forever. PARK. Stop spending attention there
— the cost of a flaky thread is not the flake, it's the attention it draws
away from live ones.

**No-show**: one message, and it's the last one.
> `hope youre alright`

Then delete the thread. Not a punishment, just accounting.

## SX — The revive

**One** attempt, at 4–7 days. Never a second. Never "hey stranger", never
"did I do something wrong", never a guilt line.

The revive that works is a **callback with no ask attached** — it proves
memory, costs her nothing to answer, and doesn't reference the silence:

> `just saw someone wearing that jacket you were defending. still wrong`

If nothing comes back, it's over, and that's information not rejection.

---

## S5 — After the first date

**Same night, if it went well**: short, specific, no debrief.
> `you were good company. doing that again`

A statement. Not "I had a really great time tonight, I'd love to see you
again if you're keen!" — that hands the frame over at the exact moment he has
it.

**Setting the second** (within 2–3 days, and make it different in kind from
the first — a walk after drinks, food after a walk):
> `saturday. theres a walk i want to show you and then food`

**She's gone quiet after a good date**: one low-cost message. Then leave it.
People are allowed to change their minds, and chasing after a good date is the
fastest way to retroactively make it a bad one.

## Common set pieces

**"What are you looking for?"** — asked early, usually a screen for whether
he's serious. His honest answer is that it depends on the person, and that
*is* the answer — delivered as confidence, not indecision:
> `depends who im talking to. im not on here for the swiping`

Evasive kills it, and so does overclaiming. Honest and unhurried beats both.

**She tests or teases him** — agree and amplify. Never defend.
> her: `you're probably a serial dater`
> him: `terrible one though, im here on a wednesday talking to you`

**She sends a photo** — react to something other than how she looks.
The content, the place, the dog, the terrible lighting. Everyone else in her
inbox went for the obvious one.

**Escalation** — tension in text should follow the temperature she's setting,
never lead it, and the real read happens in person anyway. Matching her a beat
behind is the calibration: it keeps him ahead of the pack without the cost of
misjudging it, and text is a bad instrument for this regardless. If he's
unsure whether it's too far, it is. Put the energy into getting her out
instead.
