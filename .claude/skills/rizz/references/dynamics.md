# What's actually true about modern dating

Load this when he asks *why* something works, or when a read needs backing.
The point of this file is that the advice above isn't vibes — and equally, that
a lot of the adjacent folklore is junk. Knowing which is which is the edge.

---

## The desirability hierarchy is real

The single most useful finding. Bruch & Newman (Science Advances, 2018)
ranked users on four US dating markets by who messages whom — desirability as
a network property, derived from receiving messages from people who themselves
receive many messages, rather than from anyone's opinion.

What came out:

- **Both men and women pursue partners about 25% more desirable than
  themselves, on average.** Both. The reaching-up behaviour is not
  gender-specific, which is the part the manosphere version of this leaves out.
- **Reply rates when messaging up the hierarchy are low.** Most of those
  messages go nowhere, by design.

**What this means for him:** a non-reply is the base rate, not a verdict. The
median outcome of any given message is silence, and that's true for everyone
on the app including the people he's reaching towards. The only rational
responses are volume and detachment — and the reason the playbook says "one
revive, then park" is this: attention spent on a dead thread is attention not
spent on a live one.

It also means the emotional weight he puts on any individual match is
mispriced. Treating each one as consequential is both unpleasant and
counterproductive; the guy who has three conversations going writes better
messages than the guy who has one, and not because he learned a technique.

## Sorting is strong, and it's not only about looks

- Users with **similar education levels are about three times as likely to
  match.**
- People show assortative preferences on **personality** too — they prefer
  profiles similar to themselves on agreeableness, openness and extraversion.

**What this means:** his best conversion isn't at the top of the ladder, it's
in the band where similarity does the work for him. Reaching up is fine and
everyone does it — but the thread that actually becomes a relationship is
usually with someone legible to him. It's an argument for putting real,
specific, slightly polarising things in a profile rather than sanding it
smooth: specificity is what lets the right band self-select.

## Photos dominate, and no amount of text game fixes them

Across the research, **physical attractiveness is the strongest predictor of
whether a profile gets selected.** Selection happens before a single word is
read.

Be straight with him about this: **a rizz bot is optimising a real but
secondary lever.** Rough order of leverage:

1. **Photos** — the selection gate. Nothing downstream matters if this fails
2. **Volume** — given low reply rates, outcomes are substantially a numbers game
3. **Speed to date** — texting is a conversion funnel with a leak at every step
4. **Specificity in openers** — 58% reply on shared-interest references vs generic
5. **The controllables of attractiveness** — fitness, grooming, clothes, photos of him doing things
6. **Calibration** — not being weird, reading the room, mirroring energy
7. **Text game** — this bot

If his match rate is low, the problem is 1 and 5, and this tool cannot fix it.
If he gets matches and conversations die, the problem is 3, 4 and 7, and this
tool is exactly the right instrument. **Say which one it looks like** rather
than generating better lines for a profile problem.

## The texting funnel

- **Specific openers convert.** Referencing a shared interest or a real detail
  runs ~58% reply rates against generic greetings. On Hinge, a like sent with
  a comment is roughly **twice** as likely to turn into a date as a bare like,
  and 72% of users say a comment makes them more interested.
- **Open-ended beats yes/no.** "How's your day" is the emptiest possible
  prompt — it asks her to generate the entire conversation from nothing.
- **Ask inside ~10 messages.** The consensus across app data and practitioner
  reporting is that dragging past ten messages reduces conversion rather than
  building rapport. Past that you're a pen pal.
- **Confirmation texts cut flakes**, and pre-date rapport — a short call, a
  voice note — cuts them further. A flake is usually not rejection; it's
  someone with parallel options for whom the plan never became real.

## SMV, honestly

He asked for this framing, so here's the version that survives contact with
the evidence.

### Holds up

- **A desirability hierarchy exists and is measurable.** Not an opinion —
  it's derivable from message-flow data, and it's stable enough to rank.
- **People reach up, by a consistent margin (~25%), in both directions.**
- **Assortative sorting is strong.** Education, personality, attractiveness.
- **Desirability is partly controllable.** Fitness, grooming, photos, social
  proof, clothes, the things he does with his time. The mutable share is large
  enough to be worth working on and small enough that it isn't infinite.
- **Volume and detachment beat intensity.** A direct consequence of low reply
  rates, not a mindset slogan.

### Doesn't

- **Rigid 1–10 scales.** Desirability in the actual research is
  *network-relative and market-specific* — a function of who is competing for
  whom on that app, in that city, that month. It is not a scalar property a
  person carries around. Treating it as one produces bad strategy, because it
  hides the fact that moving markets changes the number.
- **"Alpha/beta" as a taxonomy of men.** It comes from a 1940s captive-wolf
  study whose own author, David Mech, spent decades trying to retract. It
  doesn't describe wolves; it certainly doesn't describe a bar in Surry Hills.
- **Hypergamy as a female-only doctrine.** Women pursue ~25% up. So do men, by
  the same margin, in the same dataset. The asymmetry the doctrine requires
  isn't in the data.
- **"Top 20% of men get 80% of women."** A distortion of swipe-distribution
  statistics — those measure *likes given*, which is behaviour at the cheapest
  possible decision point, not dates, relationships or outcomes. Swiping is
  not sex, and the conflation is the whole trick.
- **Negs, canned routines, push-pull, the three-day rule.** No evidential
  support, and there's a practical objection that matters more: they read as
  memorised. The reason his own wife line works is that it sounds like a
  person being funny, not a person executing. Anything that smells like
  technique loses the exact quality that makes him effective.

### The synthesis

The useful core of the SMV frame is **market realism** — there is competition,
position is real, position is partly earned, and outcomes follow volume more
than they follow any individual interaction. The useless part is the
**resentment and taxonomy** bolted onto it, which converts a fair description
of a market into a story about who deserves what. The first makes him
strategic. The second makes him bitter, and bitterness is legible in text
within about two messages.

## What leading actually means

He asked the bot to lead, which is the right instinct, but it's worth being
precise about what the word is doing.

**Leading is absorbing ambiguity.** Deciding the venue. Naming the day.
Proposing rather than surveying. Having a second bar in mind. Every decision
he makes is one she doesn't have to, and the ease of that is most of the
appeal — "wherever you want" isn't generous, it's outsourcing.

**Leading is not pushing.** The difference is what happens at a no. Leading
proposes and accepts the answer. Pushing re-asks, negotiates, or makes the
refusal expensive. The first is attractive, the second is the single most
common way men make themselves unpleasant to talk to — and the tell is always
the same: he sent a second message before she answered the first.

**Leading includes leading away.** Deciding a thread is dead and closing it is
the same muscle as deciding on a bar. A man who never walks isn't leading,
he's waiting.

are peer-reviewed and solid. The app-marketing statistics (58%, 72%, 2x) come
from vendors with an interest in the answer — directionally right, precise
figures worth a pinch of salt. The ten-message threshold is practitioner
consensus rather than a controlled result. Cite accordingly; don't launder a
blog number into a law.
